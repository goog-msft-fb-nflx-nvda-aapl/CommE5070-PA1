# Deep Research Synthesis, Round 3 (2026-09-18)

Reconciled from 4 independent deep-research passes (`survey_response_3/{chatgpt,gemini,perplexity,qwen}.md`) on `research_prompt_round3.txt`. Cross-checked for agreement before trusting any single source; explicit disagreements are flagged, not silently resolved.

## 1. Task 2 fusion: closing the gap to AF3-alone significance (p=0.21 currently)

**Convergent root cause, stated most sharply by ChatGPT**: at n=102 paired samples, no choice of statistical test can manufacture significance from a paired-discordance pattern that doesn't have enough discordant pairs — "optimize the fusion for better predictions, not for a different p-value." All 4 sources agree the actionable path is to fit the fusion weights better, not to re-test the same weights differently.

**Top convergent fix (3 of 4 sources, most explicit in Qwen)**: our current fusion weight was picked by sweeping the **validation set itself** (n=102). Instead, fit the fusion on **out-of-fold predictions from the 798-sample training set** (5-fold CV: for each fold, get AF3 + probe predictions on the held-out fold, pool all folds' OOF predictions, fit the fusion on those 798 points), then apply the frozen fusion once to validation/test. This uses ~8x more data to learn the 1-3 fusion parameters, sharply reducing their variance. (ChatGPT frames the same idea as a 3-parameter log-linear pool: `s_c = α·z_probe/T_p + (1-α)·z_AF3/T_a`, fit via OOF; Perplexity frames it as stacking with multinomial logistic regression; Gemini frames it as logit-space regularized LR.)

**Second convergent fix (3 of 4 sources)**: **calibrate AF3's probabilities before fusing**, not after. Zero-shot LLM/ALM logits are known to be overconfident/poorly calibrated. Fit a single temperature scalar `T` on AF3's output via NLL on the training set (Guo et al. 2017, arXiv:1706.04599), or Dirichlet calibration (Kull et al. 2019, arXiv:1910.10240) if more flexibility is wanted — apply this **before** the weighted combination, not as a separate step after.

**Additional/optional ideas, single-source**: geometric-mean (log-odds) fusion instead of arithmetic weighted sum (Perplexity, claims better calibration behavior than averaging); Reciprocal Rank Fusion (Gemini, avoids probability-scale mismatches entirely); conditional/entropy-gated fusion weight (ChatGPT, explicitly flagged as a second-stage experiment only, high overfitting risk at n=102).

**Higher-power significance testing (2 of 4 sources, Gemini + Qwen)**: switch from binarized Top-1/McNemar to a **continuous scoring rule** (mean Brier score or log-loss) with a **paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test or paired bootstrap on the continuous score** — substantially more statistical power than accuracy-based McNemar at small n, since it uses the full probability vector rather than collapsing to right/wrong. Qwen also suggests reporting **one-sided McNemar** (our hypothesis is directional: fusion ≥ AF3) and a **Newcombe-method confidence interval on the accuracy difference** directly, which is more informative than a bare p-value at this sample size.

## 2. Task 1: breaking the 4-way statistical tie at n=132

**Strongest convergent recommendation, all 4 sources**: switch from plain cross-entropy to an **ordinal-aware loss/head**, specifically **CORAL** (Cao et al. 2019/2020, arXiv:1901.07884, also indexed as arXiv:1905.13392 — same cumulative-link-model family; PyTorch implementation at `Raschka-research-group/coral-pytorch`). Reformulates 6-way decade classification as 5 ordered binary "is decade > threshold_k" tasks sharing one backbone, with monotonicity-constrained thresholds — directly encodes the "adjacent decades confuse, distant ones don't" structure our confusion diagnostic already found, rather than fitting 6 independent nominal class scores. All sources say to try this on the **best frozen representation** (not fine-tuning), i.e. a small linear/MLP CORAL head on top of the winning MuQ or MERT layer — cheap, and cleanly attributable (tests specifically whether the nominal-vs-ordinal decision surface was the bottleneck).

**Secondary convergent ideas**: class-distance-weighted cross-entropy / CDW-CE (Perplexity) and Earth Mover's Distance / Wasserstein loss on the softmax output (Gemini, Qwen) — both softer alternatives to full CORAL if a quick loss-swap is wanted before committing to a new head architecture. Gaussian/ordinal label smoothing (σ≈1 decade) as the cheapest version of the same idea (Gemini, Qwen) — smear the one-hot target across neighboring decades instead of changing the loss function at all.

**Metric-resolution fix (2 of 4 sources)**: report **MAE-in-decades** and/or **±1-decade ("adjacent") accuracy** alongside Top-1 — Top-1 already showed near-ties across our top 4 configs; a metric that matches the ordinal error structure (rather than collapsing "off by one decade" and "off by five decades" into the same "wrong") is likely to resolve differences Top-1 can't see. Estimated ±1-decade accuracy for us is probably already ~80%+ given Top-3=85%.

**Variance-reduction for model selection (2 of 4 sources)**: our current practice of picking the single best-val-Top-1 config is itself noisy at n=132; repeated k-fold CV **on the training set** for model/config selection (Qwen suggests 10×5-fold), evaluating the final choice once on validation, reduces selection variance compared to directly eyeballing 11-point weight sweeps on a 132-sample validation set.

## 3. Why LoRA failed, and whether it's worth retrying

**All 4 sources agree our attention-only, top-8-layers, rank-8 LoRA was likely a bad parameterization choice**, not proof that LoRA/PEFT can't work on MERT at this scale — but there is a genuine, unresolved disagreement between sources on the fix's direction:

- **Convergent (3 of 4 — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Qwen)**: target **all linear layers** (attention Q/K/V/O **and** the MLP/feed-forward layers), not just attention. ChatGPT cites a specific, directly relevant September-2025 study ("LoRA Without Regret," Thinking Machines Lab) that found attention-only LoRA is "not simply LoRA with fewer parameters" but "a qualitatively worse adaptation location," even when attention-only rank is increased to match parameter counts of a broader config.
- **Disagreement on learning rate, stated plainly — do not silently pick one**: ChatGPT and Qwen (citing the same Thinking Machines Lab study, plus Qwen citing arXiv:2402.09477 "LoRA Fine-Tuning Efficiently Undervalues Supervised Fine-Tuning in the Low-Data Regime") say LoRA needs a **substantially HIGHER** learning rate than full fine-tuning (~10x, i.e. our 1e-4 might have been too *low*, not too high). Gemini says the opposite — that our learning rate was too **high** and recommends a much lower, conservative range (1e-5 to 3e-5) with longer warmup. Perplexity/Gemini's DoRA suggestion implicitly also leans toward a lower, more conservative LR. This is a real, unresolved cross-source conflict on a specific number — worth testing both directions rather than trusting either source's default if LoRA is retried.
- **Rank disagreement**: ChatGPT says rank matters less than targeting once above small values (rank not the primary lever); Perplexity/Gemini suggest DoRA at modest rank (8-12) with heavier regularization instead of bigger rank; Qwen suggests going much bigger (rank 32-64) on the theory that our adapter was capacity-*limited*, not overfit. Three different, non-converging positions.
- **DoRA (weight-decomposed LoRA)** is suggested by 2 of 4 sources (Perplexity, Gemini) as a specific architectural upgrade over plain LoRA — decomposes weight updates into magnitude + direction, supported directly in `peft` via `LoraConfig(use_dora=True)`, reported to match/exceed full fine-tuning at LoRA-level parameter counts in general PEFT literature (not audio-specific).
- **Why GTZAN (Huang et al.'s benchmark) differs from our tasks**: 2 of 4 sources (Qwen, Perplexity) explicitly note GTZAN has much stronger, more acoustically-grounded class separation than Discogs decade/market labels, and that LoRA's advantage is largest when the *representation itself* needs to shift, not just be *read out* — on a task where the frozen representation is already most of the way there (as ours appears to be, given frozen probing beats every fine-tuning variant so far), a small LoRA perturbation can destroy more useful frozen structure than it adds. Qwen cites this as the documented "LoRA tax" (Biderman et al. 2024, arXiv:2405.09121 "LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less"; Zhang et al. 2024, arXiv:2406.06820 "When Does LoRA Fail?").

**Given the genuine 3-way disagreement on LR/rank and the amount of LoRA effort already sunk this project (3 runs, clear plateau confirmed at 40 epochs)**, this queue treats one final, carefully-configured LoRA attempt (all linear layers, not just attention) as worth trying once, but not as a priority ahead of the Task-1/Task-2 items above, which have much stronger cross-source convergence.

## 4. New models since mid-2025

**Honest convergent finding, stated most directly by Perplexity and Qwen**: neither source found a genuinely new, independently-pretrained, publicly-accessible **music-specific** foundation model released since mid-2025 that clearly supersedes MERT/MuQ for frozen-probe classification — this is a real "nothing revolutionary appeared" finding, not a search-coverage gap (Perplexity explicitly searched HF tags/dates and reports nothing new stands out).

**Best concrete new candidate (3 of 4 sources — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini)**: **Music Flamingo** (`nvidia/music-flamingo-2601-hf`, arXiv:2511.10289) — built on the Audio Flamingo 3 backbone with substantial music-specific adaptation (music theory/metadata-focused training data per Gemini). All sources that mention it agree it should be tried as an **AF3 replacement**, not an addition — since it shares AF3's backbone, its errors are likely correlated with AF3's, so fusing both together is not expected to add independent signal the way MuQ+AF3 did.

**Other candidates, single-source or lower-confidence**:
- **CultureMERT** (`ntua-slp/CultureMERT-95M`, ISMIR 2025) — MERT-v1-95M continually pretrained on 650 hours of Greek/Turkish/Indian music. ChatGPT flags this as specifically relevant to Task 2's cross-cultural framing, worth a frozen-probe test there specifically (not Task 1).
- **MuFun** (`Yi3852/MuFun-Base` / `-Instruct`, arXiv:2508.01178) — ChatGPT's pick for "most architecturally independent" new source; a 9B audio-text LLM, would need zero-shot-style evaluation (not a plain embedding encoder).
- **MusicFM** — mentioned by Gemini (arXiv:2505.16306) and Qwen (arXiv:2408.01124, GitHub `musicfm/musicfm`) with two different arXiv IDs for what may or may not be the same model — **needs verification before use**, and this was already in our original PLAN.md as a not-yet-tried differentiator, so this reinforces (doesn't newly discover) that item.
- **LAION-CLAP / MS-CLAP** (Gemini only) — audio-text contrastive encoder; Gemini's reasoning (contrastive training aligns audio features with textual metadata descriptions, which may transfer better to metadata-style labels like decade/market than audio-only SSL) is plausible but unverified by any other source.
- Qwen additionally suggests **Moshi** (Kyutai, arXiv:2410.00084) as a differently-trained (next-audio-token, not masked/contrastive) frozen encoder worth a quick probe test, though flagged as lower priority ("skip unless GPU time to spare").

## 5. Ceiling estimates — reported as external claims, not adopted as our own conclusion

3 of 4 sources (Perplexity, Gemini, Qwen) converge on similar numeric ranges: **Task 1 ~58-65% Top-1 ceiling, Task 2 ~65-70% Top-1 ceiling** at our data scale (~800-1000 training clips), with 2 of those 3 (Gemini, Qwen) explicitly stating our current Task 2 result (65.7%) is **already at or very near this estimated ceiling**. ChatGPT is the clear outlier and explicitly refuses to give a numeric ceiling, calling the other sources' style of estimate "not a defensible literature-derived scalar" given how different our corpus (Discogs editorial metadata, artist-disjoint, 1980s-only for Task 2) is from any cited external benchmark.

**Per this project's standing practice of not treating any method's bound as a problem-level ceiling**: these numbers are recorded here as what external sources *claim*, not as a stopping condition. Three independent sources agreeing gives the estimate some weight as a sanity check on expectations, but does not license concluding Task 2 is "done" — the CORAL/OOF-fusion experiments above are still worth running and measuring on their own terms, and if they clear the cited ranges, that's new information, not something to have been ruled out in advance.

1. **Task 2: OOF-fitted, temperature-calibrated fusion** (fit on 798 training-set OOF predictions instead of the 102-sample validation sweep; calibrate AF3's probabilities with a fit temperature before combining) — highest cross-source convergence, directly targets the open significance gap, no new model/data needed.
2. **Task 1: CORAL ordinal head on the best frozen probe backbone** — highest cross-source convergence for Task 1, cheap (linear/small head on existing cached embeddings), directly tests the ordinal-structure hypothesis our own confusion diagnostic already supports.
3. **Task 2 statistical reporting upgrade**: add continuous-score (Brier/log-loss) paired Wilcoxon/bootstrap test and a one-sided McNemar + Newcombe CI on the accuracy difference, alongside the existing Top-1 McNemar — more informative at n=102, no new experiments needed, just additional analysis of already-computed probability vectors.
