### 5. Overfitting-Mitigation for Fine-Tuning 330M Parameters on ~800 Clips
Your observation of severe overfitting during full fine-tuning is expected. To fix this, move away from full fine-tuning and adopt Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) and regularization strategies:
* **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation):** Apply LoRA exclusively to the Query, Key, Value, and Output projection matrices of the top 4–8 transformer layers of MERT [[33]]. This reduces trainable parameters by >95%, acting as a powerful structural regularizer that prevents the model from destroying pre-trained acoustic representations.
* **Layer-Wise Learning Rate Decay (LLRD):** If you must fine-tune more than just the head, apply a decaying learning rate schedule across layers (e.g., $1\times10^{-6}$ for early layers, scaling up to $2\times10^{-5}$ for the final layers) [[41]]. This preserves the robust, general-purpose features in lower layers while allowing higher-level semantic adaptation.
* **Aggressive Audio Augmentation:** You noted using *none*. For small datasets, this is a critical vulnerability. Implement **SpecAugment** (time and frequency masking), random **pitch shifting** (±1 to 2 semitones, preserving tempo), and mild **time-stretching** (±5%). This forces the model to learn invariant production features rather than memorizing specific 10s crop artifacts.
* **Partial-Unfreeze Schedule:** Train for 5–10 epochs with the MERT backbone completely frozen (only training the classifier head). Then, unfreeze *only* the last 2 transformer layers and continue training with a very low learning rate (e.g., $5\times10^{-6}$) and high weight decay ($1\times10^{-2}$).

### 6. Current (2025–2026) Practices for Small-Data, Artist-Disjoint Classification
* **Supervised Contrastive Loss (SupCon):** Replace standard cross-entropy with a supervised contrastive loss on the MERT embeddings. SupCon explicitly pulls embeddings of the same class (e.g., "1980s US") together in the latent space while pushing different classes apart [[209]]. This is highly effective for small, artist-disjoint datasets because it learns a more robust, generalized feature space before the final classification head, reducing reliance on spurious artist-specific cues.
* **Cluster-Supervised Learning (Auxiliary Pseudo-Labeling):** Recent geographic music classification research demonstrates that applying unsupervised clustering (e.g., K-Means) to the encoder’s embeddings and using the resulting cluster assignments as an *auxiliary* loss term during supervised training can regularize the model and smooth decision boundaries [[160]]. This provides a structural prior that helps when true labels are scarce.
* **Dataset Distillation:** Emerging as a data-efficient alternative to active learning, dataset distillation synthesizes a smaller, highly informative set of "prototype" audio examples that capture the decision boundaries of the full dataset [[221]]. For a 798-sample training set, distilling this down to ~100 highly representative synthetic clips can sometimes yield better generalization than training on the raw, potentially noisy small dataset.
